# Beginner's guide — Diplomacy in 5 minutes

**The story.** Europe, spring 1901. Seven Great Powers. Everyone is polite. Everyone is lying a little. In about three years someone will own half the map — make sure it's you, or at least make sure it isn't the person who just promised to be your friend forever.

**The goal.** The map has **34 supply centres** (marked ★). Own **18** of them at the end of any Fall turn and you win outright. Most games end in an agreed draw among the survivors.

**Your pieces.** Armies (♟) travel on land. Fleets (⚓) travel on water and coasts. All pieces are equally strong: strength **1**, always. Numbers come from teamwork, not from bigger pieces.

**A year has three beats.**
1. **Spring** — talk, then everyone secretly submits orders; all moves happen at once.
2. **Fall** — talk again, move again. Centres change hands to whoever is standing on them at the end of Fall.
3. **Winter** — if you own more centres than pieces, build new pieces on your empty home centres. If you own fewer, remove the excess.

**The only four orders you'll ever give**
- **Hold** — stay put and defend.
- **Move** — step into a neighbouring province.
- **Support** — stay put and add +1 strength to another unit's move or hold. The supported action must be one you could have moved into yourself. You *can* support other players' units — that's how alliances become real.
- **Convoy** — a Fleet sitting in a sea carries an Army from one coast across that sea to another coast. Chains of fleets can carry an army across several seas.

**How fights work (no dice, ever)**
- Attack strength = 1 + supports for that move. Defence strength = 1 + supports for that hold.
- Strictly greater wins; the loser is *dislodged* and must retreat to an empty neighbouring province it can enter (not where the attacker came from), or be removed.
- Equal strength = **bounce**: nobody moves. Two units ordered into each other's provinces also bounce (they don't swap).
- **Cut support:** if a supporting unit is itself attacked from any province *other than* the one its support is aimed at, its support doesn't count.

**Talking is the game.** Orders are physics; press is politics. Coordinate supports and convoys, trade centres, form a wall against the leader — and remember that **no promise is binding**. A betrayal timed one season early is a disaster; one season late is a tragedy; on time, it's Diplomacy.

**Your first three turns (any power)**
1. Spring 1901: move toward the neutral centres nearest you. Tell the two powers who can hurt you most what you're doing before you do it.
2. Fall 1901: land on those centres. Ask one neighbour for one specific support — small, concrete asks get yeses.
3. Winter 1901: build where the next fight will be, not where the last one was.

**Use the training wheels — they're good.** The 💡 Hints tab watches your position and flags free centres, doomed solo attacks, undefended home centres and support opportunities. The 🤖 AI Strategist (optional, in Settings) reads the whole board and proposes actual orders. The Press 🎭 persona button makes your messages sound like the movies — fun, and weirdly, people reply faster to a Bond villain.

Full rules reference: the in-app Hints + this guide cover ~95% of games. The remaining 5% (convoys under attack, paradoxes) is what the DATC test cases exist for — see the README's honesty section for what this edition simplifies.
